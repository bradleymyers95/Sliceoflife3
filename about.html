Why we started this

Travel got good at showing you things. It got worse at introducing you to people.

Slice Of Life is a small platform built on one idea: the best way to understand a place is to take part in an ordinary day there, alongside someone who lives it.

The problem with "experiences"

Somewhere along the way, "authentic" became a marketing word — a staged cooking class, a rehearsed folk show, a market tour narrated for tourists. It looks like culture, but it's a performance built for outsiders, and everyone involved knows it.

We wanted the opposite: the recipe someone's grandmother actually makes on Sundays. The clean-up crew that meets whether or not visitors show up. The craft that was already being taught before anyone thought to charge for it.

What we built instead

A place where local people list what they're already doing, and travelers ask to join in. No professional guides, no fixed script, no group of forty. Just an invitation, sized for a handful of people, priced to cover what it actually costs.

What we believe

A short list of things we won't compromise on

Belief 01

Hospitality isn't a service

Hosts aren't employees, and guests aren't customers in the usual sense. This works best when both sides treat it as a genuine exchange.

Belief 02

Small is a feature

We'll never push hosts to scale up group sizes for more revenue. A kitchen table only fits so many people, and that's the point.

Belief 03

Money should stay honest

We charge one small flat fee to keep the lights on. No dynamic pricing, no hidden markups on what a host asks for.
